## Four moving parts and one loop. That is the whole engine.

```
                      ┌───────────────┐
        ┌────────────►│   BUILDINGS   │◄──────────────┐
        │             └───────┬───────┘               │
        │                     │                       │
   people work them    ┌──────┴──────┐        coin builds them
        │              ▼             ▼                │
        │          ┌──────┐      ┌───────┐            │
        │          │ FOOD │      │ CARGO │            │
        │          └───┬──┘      └───┬───┘            │
        │              │             │                │
        │       sets the ceiling   sold at            │
        │              │           market             │
        │              ▼             ▼                │
        │        ┌──────────┐   ┌────────┐            │
        └────────┤  PEOPLE  │   │  COIN  ├────────────┘
                 └────┬─────┘   └────┬───┘
                      │              ▲
                      └──────────────┘
                        upkeep drains it
```

**Read it as a sentence.** Buildings make food and cargo · food decides how many people the town can
hold · people are the hands that run the buildings · cargo sells for coin · coin builds more
buildings · people cost money to keep.

## The three phases a town runs, each season

| | |
|---|---|
| **PRODUCE** | Huts and garden plots appear on their own as the town grows — **free, and they are why a sacked town crawls back.** Hands are assigned, best work first: **food, then guns, then earnings.** Anything unmanned goes idle. |
| **SETTLE** | **Bread before batteries** — food is bought before anything else can be spent. What is left is banked. Standing drifts slowly toward what the ledger says; population drifts toward what the town can now feed. |
| **GOVERN** | The town may claim one more patch of ground, and the governor makes **one** decision — and publishes his reason, or his refusal, either way. |

## What the ground does, and what it never does

> **A tile is a multiplier on a baseline the town already has — never a source.**
> Claiming timbered hills does not give a town wood. It gives it **more** wood, and the extra is
> what goes to market.

- **The ground decides what a town SELLS.** Suitability, the sweet spot and distance all scale cargo.
- **The ground has nothing to say about what a town EATS.** Every town feeds the same flat base off
  the same works, wherever it was founded.
- **So a town can never starve on bad dirt**, and every food building is guaranteed to feed more
  people than it takes to run.

**Growth past that base is bought, not grown.** A great port is great because it buys most of its
bread — measured, the largest ports buy **50–77%** of what they eat. **Burn the quay and that stops.**

## What pushes on it from outside

| | does what |
|---|---|
| **Weather** | spoils a harvest · breaks a building · sends a repair bill |
| **The market** | sets prices. No town controls them. |
| **Access** | how much of the world it can reach — its own quays plus lanes still open. **Cut it and trade stops.** |
| **A patron** | money from outside the loop entirely |

---

## The three caps that bind, and nothing else does

**Coin** — it has to afford the thing · **Land** — a region has only so many hexes · **The menu** —
one tavern is enough, two is plenty.

There is **no materials gate**: a town is never short of timber for its own building. That was
deleted, and with it the rule that a sawpit was exempt from needing planks.

---

## What is not built

- **Damage has no degrees.** A building is whole or gone; nothing mends, nothing takes time to raise.
- **A town cannot fail out loud.** It can drift to nobody, and nothing says so.
- **Nobody takes the purse.** The coin is there to be looted; no raid exists yet to loot it.
